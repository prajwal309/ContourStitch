# Progressive multi-view measurement

The new engine is integrated into `/measure`. Enable it with `CONTOURSTITCH_PROGRESSIVE_ENGINE=1` in a development or preview environment. The fixed-pose implementation remains the default while the new engine awaits independent tape and device validation. The public workflow offers Camera-Based Measurement and Enter Measurements Manually. The legacy comparison UI and benchmark dashboard are accessible through their existing routes in development. In production those routes redirect to the measurement page.

## Audit and design choices

| Existing path | Reused or retained | Where it breaks down |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed-pose live silhouette estimator | Landmark paths, centered silhouette spans, arm-occlusion checks, neck sampling, circumference code, consent and results UI | Requires an uncropped body and a held sequence. Width and depth use discrete poses and rejects some distance changes. |
| Rotation geometric baseline | Retained for comparison. It helped identify region-relative scale and angle diversity as useful components. | Requires full-body evidence and a directed 360° turn. A single distinct-pose convention limits what it can use. Its angle origin is 180° offset from the live path on the same synthetic landmarks. |
| Voxel visual hull | Retained as an internal reference. Its interactive mesh renderer is reused for the progressive section model. | Normalizes whole silhouettes. Truncated fields of view and pose changes can remove geometry during voxel carving. Runtime grows with grid size and number of views, and shape hidden in concavities remains unobservable. [Laurentini’s visual hull paper](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/273735/) |
| Parametric fitting option | Retained as a development comparison target with its existing unavailable status. The bundled synthetic ONNX regression is a separate experimental feature predictor. | The parametric option has no weights, fitting implementation, or service. No valid comparative outputs are available. [SMPL model license](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html) |

The preview uses a small geometric model because its parameters and failure conditions can be inspected. A calibrated perspective bundle adjustment would require reliable camera intrinsics or additional scale and depth constraints. The inferred landmark depth provides yaw cues. Its units alone cannot establish metric scale. A Bayesian posterior or a licensed parametric body model may become useful when representative evidence and a calibrated residual model are available. The current implementation uses empirical repeatability gates and does not assign probability to convergence. [MediaPipe Pose Landmarker](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker?tab=Web)

## Geometry and data flow

1. The consent-gated webcam sends frames to the existing local MediaPipe pose and segmentation model. Browser permission, camera switching, interruption handling, and track cleanup use the established camera flow.
2. `multiview/observe.ts` maps each usable frame to visible shoulder–hip and hip–knee landmarks, a yaw estimate, and silhouettes at landmark-relative chest, waist, hip, and neck levels. It uses contiguous foreground spans attached to the estimated section center. It drops spans touching the image boundary and samples touching projected arm paths where applicable. Low light, blur, a missing or unstable mask, and multiple detected people exclude a frame. Only observed regions contribute to fusion.
3. Under weak perspective, the image positions of neighboring body points can be written as a translated and rotated projection with local scale `s_t ≈ f / Z_t`. Each frame divides its projected widths and paths by the visible vertical anchor span, which cancels image translation and approximately cancels `s_t`. Yaw comes from the signed shoulder or hip axis. Local anchors are rejected when too small or when their apparent lateral lean exceeds the gate. This accommodates changing apparent scale within the weak-perspective regime. Strong perspective, lens distortion, unequal segment depth, and articulation remain sources of error.
4. `multiview/fusion.ts` retains one bounded buffer per measurement, anchor, and 15° yaw bin. A bin keeps at most five temporally separated readings and can replace a lower-quality one. Bin medians and spread are cached until evidence changes. Rotations with large inter-frame yaw jumps are skipped. Missing or cropped regions leave the retained evidence alone.
5. For each section, shared body parameters are frontal diameter `a` and sagittal diameter `b` in anchor-relative units. With yaw `θ_t`, the section model predicts `w_t² = a² cos²(θ_t) + b² sin²(θ_t)`. A two-view hypothesis supplies an initial fit. Distinct views vote within a robust relative-width residual gate, then quality-weighted least squares refits the squared widths. Positive axes and separation in `cos²(θ)` are required. Repeatable views must span distinct axes before a circumference becomes acquired. The app converts the fitted axes into circumference using the existing Ramanujan ellipse approximation. Paths use repeated near-frontal landmark distances with robust agreement across views. Torso shape is constrained to ellipses, and the mesh interpolates between the observed torso rings. It omits unobserved body parts.
6. Scale comes from the entered stature plus repeatable uncropped stature evidence, or from a known-length object positioned upright in the subject’s plane beside visible landmarks. The stature route can establish shoulder–hip and hip–knee anchors after partial observations have already been collected. A torso-only or thigh-only reference can supply one anchor directly. There is no automatic metric conversion when neither scale route succeeds. Relative geometry stays in memory. Height-conditioned ANSUR II values remain separately labeled references where supported and cannot supply a saved camera measurement. Inseam remains manual because this path has no supported crotch-to-floor measurement. Outseam uses a hip–knee–ankle landmark proxy.
7. `multiview/planner.ts` scores missing frontal or lateral evidence, regional visibility, calibration needs, and unresolved limb paths. Front, side, arm, and leg guidance can be requested in any order. The preview marks each dimension Estimating, Refining, Acquired, or Insufficient Data. Progress counts acquisition against supported camera measurements. Repeatability ranges come from retained width/path spread and fit residuals and are uncalibrated. Saved camera reports only include supported, metric, acquired values with `uncertaintyCm: null`.

The worker extracts numeric observations from one transferable mask copy at a time. The main thread keeps bounded fusion and guidance state and uses inline extraction if the worker cannot run. MediaPipe remains WebAssembly-backed with its existing CPU delegate, and the browser preview is decoupled from the sampling timer. The preview targets at most about three inference frames per second before processing overhead, with no reconstruction queue. The development panel shows recent detector and geometry time, worker transfer overhead included, as well as processed-frame cadence. Frame and mask data are transient and are not logged, sent to a backend, or stored in a profile. Closing or interrupting the scan terminates the worker and releases the stream.

## Reproduce the benchmark

```sh
CONTOURSTITCH_BENCHMARK_OUTPUT=/tmp/contourstitch-multiview.json npm run benchmark:multiview
CONTOURSTITCH_PROGRESSIVE_ENGINE=1 npm run test:e2e -- e2e/multiview.spec.ts
```

The synthetic harness projects three known elliptical body volumes and corresponding landmarks. Every runnable engine receives identical input timelines, with repeated views and a continuous rotation. Nine scenarios cover full framing, upper and lower crops, permanent scale ambiguity, apparent distance and position changes, occlusion/missing landmarks, expanded clothing silhouettes, lighting/blur gates, and resolution. It compares chest, waist, and hip against numerical integration of the ground-truth cross-sections. The legacy paths sample different anatomical levels, which contributes to differences. The synthetic volumes favor the progressive engine's ellipse model. These results are suitable for checking geometry, coverage and robustness under controlled conditions. They do not measure wearer accuracy, actual visual detector robustness, or equivalence to tape conventions.

The progressive engine acquired 21/27 chests, 21/27 waists, and 24/27 hips, with synthetic MAEs of 3.20, 3.03, and 3.07 cm respectively. It resolved all nine clean and upper-crop cases and all three hips in the lower-crop sequence. It withheld every estimate in the permanently cropped, unscaled sequence. Mean error was 13.77 cm for the deliberately enlarged clothing silhouette. Additional frames cannot resolve a systematic difference between a body and the outside of its clothing. The visual hull results flag disagreement with this fixture and its reference levels. They by themselves cannot distinguish a volume, pose registration, projection or circumference-sampling fault.

The JSON output contains actual errors, per-scenario and per-measurement summaries, acquisition times and processing times. An Apple M1/Node 22 run recorded a median of 7.65 seconds on the simulated timeline to the first qualifying estimate per acquired dimension, and 0.77 ms median per-session P95 for progressive geometry on ready-made masks, excluding webcam, browser, and MediaPipe inference. These are geometry timings. Session processing times include per-frame geometry and final reconstruction where applicable. The bench records process-wide CPU time and peak RSS separately. Browser FPS, GPU utilization, real-device memory, real-world repeatability, and uncertainty coverage remain unmeasured.

Partial capture increased coverage by 12 measurements in this workload. Intermediate yaw offered a small error reduction with the same final coverage. Display priors changed no saved prediction. Separate end-to-end studies are still needed to isolate temporal gating, keyframe selection, visibility checks, and guidance. Full parametric fitting and learned anatomical regularization were left out of this implementation and have no ablation results.

## Release validation and licenses

Collect consented, de-identified repeated webcam sessions with independent tape references on held-out people and devices. Prespecify per-measurement tolerances and acceptable failure rates with the garment team. Stratify errors by shape, clothing, camera angle and browser. Measure within-person repeatability, camera and reconstruction timing, memory, and calibration coverage separately. Benchmark alternative instructions with people following them, since replaying fixed frames cannot estimate the effect of adaptive guidance. Compare outputs on matched cases and report withheld measurements independently of error. Use hardware profiles before changing the MediaPipe delegate, trying WebGPU, or moving detector inference into a worker.

The preview adds no third-party library or model weights. The installed MediaPipe Tasks Vision SDK is Apache-2.0. Verify the provenance and redistribution terms for the specific deployed model bundle and keep its notices with the assets. Review the ANSUR II dataset terms separately from the SDK before expanding its use. The existing ONNX Runtime package used in the internal path lists the MIT license. SMPL’s academic model license excludes commercial deployment without separate permission.
